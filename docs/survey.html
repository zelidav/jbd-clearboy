The Survey

Ten photographs with a stainless rule in frame, resolved into a dimensioned survey and rebuilt as a watertight CAD solid. Every mockup is downstream of these numbers.

01

How this piece gets made

Bench notes

Stock — borosilicate 3.3

Everything here is COE 33 boro: 3.3 × 10−6/K expansion, which is what lets a piece take a lighter and a cold sink without checking. The original stem came straight off stock tube — 11 OD, 8 ID. The mockups run it out to ∅14 so the enamel label has something to sit on.

density 2.23 g/cm³ · working ≈ 1250 °C · softening ≈ 820 °C

Head — blown and shaped

The chamber starts as a length of heavy tube, closed at one end, heated to a gather and blown out against the shape the maker wants. Marvered, waisted, worked until the lobe reads full and the bowl end tapers. Hand-shaped means slightly oval, always.

42 × 37 section · 68 long · wall ≈ 3 mm (inferred)

Bowl — pushed in from the rim

The funnel is pushed into the hot chamber wall rather than added on — the technique the Eugene scene traces back to Bob Snodgrass, and the reason a bowl like this has no seam. It necks from ∅25 at the rim down to a ∅5 throat.

∅25 rim → ∅5 throat · 18–20 deep

Frit — rolled, not painted

The hot end is rolled through crushed colour on the marver so the grains stick and melt in, leaving a granular band that catches light differently from the body. The marbles go on over it, pressed in while everything is still soft.

band ≈ 25 mm · grains 0.6–1.3 mm · 4 marbles on the hammer, 7 at the jar rim

Fume — silver and gold vapour

Silver reads blue-violet, gold reads pink-amber. Both go on as a coat a few atoms thick, which is why the colour moves with the angle you hold it at — and why it keeps moving with use. These renders are the day-one state, not year two.

silver → blue / violet · gold → pink / amber

Anneal — the part that decides

Off the torch it goes straight into the kiln: soak near the annealing point to let the stress out, then a slow ramp down through the strain point. Rush it and a piece with a 3 mm chamber wall against a 7 mm foot cracks weeks later, at rest.

anneal ≈ 560 °C · strain ≈ 518 °C · slow ramp

Before tooling

Wall thickness is the one group that was never measured — it was read off highlights and reasoned from the form. Two caliper readings, on the rim and on the stem OD, lock the entire model. Everything downstream of those numbers (mass, volume, glass cost, print scaling) moves if they come back different.
